1. Executive summary
The challenge asks for two ordered top-10 lists: batters and bowlers who perform reliably in high-pressure match situations. The model uses only IPL and T20I ball-by-ball records from 2022
onwards, links each player's pressure output to a comparable non-pressure baseline, and applies minimum sample requirements plus a reliability adjustment.
The selected match metadata contains 853 IPL/T20I matches. Ball-by-ball records are available for 837 of these: 367 IPL matches and 470 T20I matches. The analysis uses 193,988 delivery
records from the first two innings. Super overs are excluded. Sixteen metadata matches have no corresponding ball-by-ball rows and therefore cannot contribute to player-level calculations.

2. Data preparation and audit
The competition table was joined to matches by comp_id, and matches were joined to ball-by-ball records by match_id. Only competition names beginning with IPL and rows marked T20I were
retained. Other domestic/franchise T20 competitions were excluded even where match_format was T20. The 2022–2026 window is inherited from the supplied competition dataset.
Delivery-level team runs were reconstructed as batter runs plus extras1_runs plus extras_2_runs. Legal balls exclude wides and Overstep/no-ball records. Dismissals are attributed using
dismissed_player_id; bowler wickets are restricted to Bowled, Caught, LBW, Stump and Hit Wicket. Run-outs and other non-bowler dismissals do not count as bowler wickets.
Data-quality checks found no duplicate delivery keys (match_id, innings, overid, over_ball_num), and no missing striker or bowler IDs in the analysed first and second innings. Twenty-three
matches contain revised DLS targets. Where available, revised_target and the supplied revised innings ball limit are used; otherwise the model defaults to 120 balls. Progress and innings phase
are calculated relative to the effective innings length.

3. Defining pressure
Pressure is role-specific. A batter is under pressure when the batting side faces a demanding chase or a late-innings wicket constraint. A bowler is under pressure when the opposition retains
enough wickets to accelerate late in the first innings, or when the chase is still competitive in the second innings. This avoids treating every death-over delivery as equally pressurised for both roles.
Let progress = legal balls bowled before the delivery ÷ effective innings ball limit. Wickets remaining = 10 − wickets lost before the delivery. Required run rate (RRR) = runs needed × 6 ÷ balls
remaining.
Batter pressure rules
Second innings: progress ≥ 50% and at least one of the following holds: (a) RRR ≥ 8.5; (b) wickets remaining ≤ 5 and RRR ≥ 7.5; or (c) progress ≥ 75% and RRR is between 6 and 12. First innings:
(a) progress ≥ 75% and at least 4 wickets have fallen; or (b) progress is 50–75% and at least 6 wickets have fallen.
Bowler pressure rules
Second innings: progress ≥ 50%, at least 3 wickets remain, and RRR is between 6.5 and 12. First innings: (a) progress ≥ 75% and at least 4 wickets remain; or (b) progress is 50–75% and at least
6 wickets remain.
Why the rules are meaningful
The thresholds combine match stage, scoring demand and wickets in hand. A very high RRR can place the batting side under pressure; a close chase with wickets available can place the bowling
side under pressure. In the first innings, the batter and bowler rules intentionally differ because a batting collapse and a strong late-innings platform create different pressures for the two roles.

4. Performance measurement and scoring
Player baselines are matched by innings number and phase (Powerplay, Middle, Death). For every player, a pressure delivery is compared with the player's non-pressure deliveries in the same
innings number and phase. This controls for the fact that death overs naturally have different scoring and bowling conditions from powerplay or middle overs.
Batting metrics
Pressure strike rate = 100 × pressure batter runs ÷ pressure balls faced. Pressure average = pressure batter runs ÷ pressure dismissals. Boundary rate = pressure fours and sixes ÷ pressure balls.
Improvement metrics are pressure strike rate minus the matched baseline strike rate, and pressure average minus the matched baseline average. Dismissals include a batter dismissed as striker
or non-striker; balls faced and runs remain attributed to the striker.
Bowling metrics


=== PAGE 4 ===
Pressure economy = 6 × bowler-conceded runs ÷ legal balls bowled. Byes, leg-byes and penalty runs are excluded from bowler-conceded runs; wides and no-ball extras are charged to the bowler.
Bowler wickets include Bowled, Caught, LBW, Stump and Hit Wicket. Wicket rate is wickets per 100 legal balls; dot-ball rate is dot balls ÷ legal balls. Improvements are matched-baseline economy
minus pressure economy, and pressure wicket rate minus matched-baseline wicket rate.

5. SPRI formula and eligibility
Each metric is converted to a percentile rank across the eligible player pool. Higher values are better except economy, where lower is better. Percentiles make metrics on different scales
comparable and reward relative standing rather than arbitrary raw-unit additions.
Batter raw score = 0.25 × Pctl(pressure SR) + 0.25 × Pctl(∆SR) + 0.20 × Pctl(pressure average) + 0.15 × Pctl(∆average) + 0.15 × Pctl(boundary rate).
Bowler raw score = 0.25 × Pctl(−pressure economy) + 0.25 × Pctl(economy improvement) + 0.20 × Pctl(pressure wickets/100 balls) + 0.15 × Pctl(∆wickets/100 balls) + 0.15 × Pctl(dot-ball rate).
Reliability adjustment = √[pressure balls ÷ (pressure balls + 120)] × √[pressure matches ÷ (pressure matches + 10)]. Final SPRI = 100 × raw score × (0.65 + 0.35 × reliability adjustment). The
adjustment moderates small samples without allowing volume alone to determine the ranking.
Minimum eligibility
Batters: at least 120 pressure balls, 12 pressure matches, 8 dismissals and 60 matched baseline balls. Bowlers: at least 120 pressure legal balls, 12 pressure matches and 60 matched baseline
balls. These thresholds provide a meaningful sample while retaining a sufficient candidate pool for a top-10 list.
Sensitivity check
Raising the thresholds to 150 pressure balls and 15 pressure matches (plus 10 dismissals for batters) retained all 10 batter selections and 8 of the 10 bowler selections. This suggests the batting
list is especially stable to stricter volume eligibility; the two less-stable bowling places should be treated with comparatively lower confidence.

8. Limitations and appropriate use
1. Pressure thresholds are judgement-based. Different RRR, wicket and match-stage thresholds may change rankings. The report discloses the rules so they can be challenged and reproduced.
2. The dataset spans IPL and T20I. Player baselines are matched by innings and phase, but competition strength, venues, opposition quality, pitch conditions and role differences are not fully
modelled. A player whose data is mostly T20I should not be treated as having demonstrated the same IPL-specific pressure record as an IPL regular.
3. Pressure average can be volatile and depends on dismissal attribution and opportunities. The model uses minimum dismissals and sample reliability, but it cannot remove all role-related bias
(for example, a finisher often has fewer balls and fewer dismissal opportunities than a top-order batter).
4. The bowler wicket list uses only dismissals credited to the bowler. The dataset's extras classification may contain rare 'other' cases whose exact scoring treatment should be verified against the
original provider documentation if available.
5. SPRI is a relative score, not a win-probability model, player valuation, auction price estimate, availability check or guarantee of future performance. The supplied files do not establish 2027
auction availability, current contracts, player fitness or Rajasthan Royals' budget and squad vacancies.
6. The ranking is not a claim that every selected player is a suitable RR purchase. It answers the Round 1 analytical question: who has produced the strongest pressure-performance profile in the
supplied IPL/T20I data.
